It's the first thing most people ask me before they book. Here's the honest version: no hype, no fear-mongering, and what the official advisories actually say.

## What the official advisories say

- **United States:** Mexico overall is at **Level 2 — Exercise increased caution**. The advisory breaks the country down by state. **Quintana Roo** — Cancún, Playa del Carmen, Tulum, Cozumel and Isla Mujeres — is also at **Level 2**. Several other Mexican states are at Level 3 or 4, which is why "Mexico" headlines can sound scarier than the situation in Cancún. The advisory was last updated on May 29, 2026.
- **Canada:** the national advice is **"Exercise a high degree of caution."** As of its November 2026 update, Quintana Roo is **not** on Canada's list of regions to avoid. Canada did briefly add Quintana Roo to its "avoid non-essential travel" list in February 2026, then changed its advice again — so always check the latest version before you fly.

Level 2 doesn't mean "dangerous." It means "be smart." It's the same level the US gives many popular destinations in Europe.

## Area by area

### Hotel Zone
This is where most visitors stay: the 22 km strip of resorts. It's busy, well lit and full of tourists and police. The main risks here are **overcharging, timeshare pitches, drink spiking in clubs and strong currents** at some beaches.

### Downtown Cancún
Where locals live, and where you'll find real Mexican food. Busy central spots like **Parque de las Palapas** are lively in the evening. Like any city, avoid empty streets late at night and take a taxi or Uber between places.

### Costa Mujeres & Playa Mujeres
Newer, quieter resort areas north of Cancún. Most visitors here stay inside their resort or take organised transport.

### Playa del Carmen & Tulum
Popular, walkable and busy with tourists, especially Quinta Avenida in Playa and the beach clubs in Tulum. Use the same precautions as in the Hotel Zone, especially at night and in nightlife areas.

## The real risks (and how to avoid them)

- **Scams and overcharging.** Taxis without an agreed price, "free" gifts that turn into timeshare pitches, and bills with a tip added without asking. Use my [fair price checker](/tools/fair-prices) before you pay, and walk straight past the sellers at the airport ([here's how](/guides/cancun-airport)).
- **Drink spiking.** Never leave a drink unattended, and be careful with drinks from people you just met. This is most likely in nightclubs and bars.
- **ATMs.** Use ATMs inside banks or shops, cover the keypad, and don't take out large amounts at once. Avoid changing big sums in public areas of the airport.
- **Taxis.** Use a pre-booked transfer, a reputable taxi or a ride-hailing app instead of hailing random cars. Agree on the price before you get in.
- **The ocean.** Some beaches have strong currents and no lifeguards. Check the flag colour and don't swim after drinking.
- **Balconies.** Railings in Mexico are often lower than at home. Canada's advice specifically warns about falls — take care, especially with kids.

## My bottom line

Cancún is one of the most visited places in the Americas, and the vast majority of trips go smoothly. Book your airport transfer before you land, stay in the main areas, keep the same common sense you'd use in any big city, and check the advisories right before you travel — they change.
