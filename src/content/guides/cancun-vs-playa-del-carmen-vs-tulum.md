All three are on the same stretch of Caribbean coast, all three are reached from Cancún airport, and people mix them up all the time. But they feel like three different trips. I live in Cancún and spend a lot of time down the coast, so this is how I help friends choose.

## The quick difference

| | Cancún | Playa del Carmen | Tulum |
|---|---|---|---|
| From Cancún airport | 20–35 min to the Hotel Zone | About 45–60 min (55 km) | About 1.5–2 hours (120 km) |
| Hotels | Big resorts and all-inclusives | Mix of resorts and small hotels | Mostly small boutique hotels |
| Walkable? | Only in parts | Yes, around 5th Avenue | Not really, you'll need rides |
| Best for | Beach week, families, first-timers | Walking around, day trips | Ruins, cenotes, a slower pace |
| Price feel | Widest range | Mid-range | Highest for what you get |

The rest of this guide is about the trade-offs behind that table.

## Cancún: easiest beach vacation

Cancún is the one I'd pick for most first trips. The airport is close, so you can be in the pool a couple of hours after landing, and there are more all-inclusive resorts here than anywhere else on the coast. If the plan is "beach, pool, eat, repeat", this is the simplest way to do it.

The Hotel Zone is a long strip, and where you stay matters: calm bay water on the north side, the clubs in the middle, wider beaches and bigger waves to the south. I break that down, plus whether downtown is worth it, in my [Hotel Zone vs downtown guide](/guides/hotel-zone-vs-downtown-cancun).

Two of my own stays show the range. The [Hilton Cancún Mar Caribe](/hotels/hilton-cancun-mar-caribe) is a polished family all-inclusive; I scored it 9.3. For couples, [Haven Riviera Cancún](/hotels/haven-riviera-cancun) just south of the Hotel Zone is adults-only, with service I gave a 10, and being close to the airport makes your last day easy.

**The downside:** most of the Hotel Zone is built for cars and resorts, not strolling. If you want to walk out the door to a different restaurant every night, Cancún makes you work for it.

## Playa del Carmen: walkable and in the middle

Playa is a real beach town you can walk. 5th Avenue (Quinta Avenida) runs parallel to the beach for blocks, lined with restaurants, bars and shops, and the hotels are close to it. It suits couples, friends and anyone who gets bored inside a resort.

It also sits in the middle of everything. The ferry to Cozumel leaves from Playa, which is how most people do the [Cozumel reef snorkel](/tours/cozumel-snorkel). The big parks are close too: [Xel-Há](/tours/xel-ha-park) is about 45 minutes from Playa, and the [Xcaret park](/tours/xcaret-park) is just south of town.

If you like resorts, there are big ones just outside Playa. At [Hotel Xcaret Arte](/hotels/hotel-xcaret-arte) the activities got a 10 from me, the best of any hotel in the region, but at around $1,300 a night I don't think it's worth it unless you'll really use the parks. Further south, toward Tulum, the [Barceló Maya Riviera](/hotels/barcelo-maya-riviera) was a clear "definitely recommended" for the price, with a huge beach and one of the biggest pools on the coast.

**The downside:** the beaches in town get busy, and seaweed can be a problem on this part of the coast in the warm months. Check my [sargassum season guide](/guides/cancun-sargassum-season) before you book.

## Tulum: ruins, cenotes and boutique hotels

Tulum is the most different of the three. The Mayan ruins sit on a cliff above the sea, there are cenotes all around, and the beach road is lined with small hotels and beach clubs rather than big resorts. It's the pick for people who want a slower, more nature-focused trip.

My honest take on the ruins: they're small, and an hour is enough. Pair them with a beach or a cenote and you have a full day. Entrance fees for the ruins and the national park around them changed more than once in 2026, so check the current price before you go and bring pesos.

**The downsides:** it's the farthest from Cancún airport, it's the least walkable (town and beach are separate, so you'll be using taxis, bikes or a car), and prices are high for what you get. There are a few all-inclusives in the area, but far fewer than in Cancún.

One thing that helps now: **Tulum has its own airport (TQO)**. It opened in December 2023, about 40 minutes from central Tulum, and several US airlines fly there nonstop. If Tulum is your base, compare flights there before defaulting to Cancún.

## Getting between them

- **Car or private transfer:** everything is on Highway 307. Cancún airport to Playa is about 45–60 minutes; to Tulum, 1.5–2 hours. Traffic and roadworks can stretch that a lot. For arrivals, a pre-booked transfer is the least stressful option, and I explain why in my [airport guide](/guides/cancun-airport-to-hotel-zone).
- **ADO bus:** clean, air-conditioned, and runs from all Cancún airport terminals to Playa del Carmen and Tulum. Buses to Playa leave about every 30 minutes; Tulum has fewer departures, and the ride takes about 2.5 hours. Check today's fares and times on ado.com.mx.
- **Tren Maya:** the train has stations in Cancún, Playa del Carmen, Tulum and at Tulum airport. As of October 2026, the international fare was 232 pesos from Cancún to Playa and 246 pesos from Playa to Tulum. But the Cancún–Playa leg only restarted in August 2026 with very few trains a day, so check trenmaya.gob.mx before you plan around it.

## My recommendation

- **First trip, families, or a pure beach-and-pool week:** Cancún. Shortest transfer, most choice.
- **Couples or friends who like to walk out for dinner, or want Cozumel and the parks:** Playa del Carmen.
- **Ruins, cenotes, yoga, small hotels, and a bigger budget:** Tulum, ideally flying into TQO.
- **Can't decide?** Stay in Cancún or Playa and do Tulum as a day trip. The [Tulum ruins and beach tour](/tours/tulum-ruins-and-beach) or a [cenote tour](/tours/cenote-tour) gives you a taste without moving hotels. My [hotel match tool](/tools/hotel-match) can help with the rest.
