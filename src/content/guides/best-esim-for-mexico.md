Having data the moment you land in Cancún matters more than people think. You need it to message your transfer driver, open Google Maps, order an Uber later in the trip, and translate a menu. Here's how to sort it out before you fly — and when you **don't** need to spend anything.

## Step 1: Check if your plan already covers Mexico

This is the part most "best eSIM" articles skip, because they want to sell you one.

- **T-Mobile (US):** most current plans include **unlimited talk and text in Mexico** plus a chunk of **high-speed data** — 5, 10 or 15 GB depending on the plan. After that, data still works but slows way down. Check your plan in the T-Life app.
- **AT&T (US):** all four current unlimited plans (Value, Extra, Premium and Elite) include **talk, text and data in Mexico**. Older or non-unlimited plans may need the International Day Pass, which costs extra every day.
- **Verizon (US):** depends on your plan. Some include Mexico, others charge a daily TravelPass. Check before you go.
- **Canadian carriers:** roaming in Mexico usually costs extra (for example, Rogers doesn't include Mexico in its plans and sells add-ons instead). This is where an eSIM really pays off.

If your plan already includes Mexico with enough data for your trip, you're done. Skip the rest.

## Step 2: If it doesn't, get an eSIM

An eSIM is a digital SIM card. You buy it online, install it at home with a QR code, and switch it on when you land. Your normal number stays on your phone for bank texts and calls.

## Step 3 (for longer stays): a local Telcel SIM

If you're staying for weeks, you can buy a prepaid **Telcel** SIM card ("chip") at an OXXO convenience store or a Telcel shop and add credit. It gives you a Mexican number, and Telcel has the widest coverage in the country. It takes more effort than an eSIM, so for a one-week vacation I wouldn't bother.

## How to set up an eSIM (2 minutes)

1. Make sure your phone is **unlocked** and supports eSIM.
2. Buy the eSIM **at home** and scan the QR code while you're on Wi-Fi.
3. Leave it **off** until you land.
4. When the plane lands, switch the eSIM on, turn on **data roaming for that eSIM only**, and set it as your data line.
